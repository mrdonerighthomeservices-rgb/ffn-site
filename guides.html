Adult Guide Program

Paid Instruction for Grown Anglers, and How Every Guide Is Screened

Teaching a forty-year-old to read water is not a consolation prize. This is a separate, adult-only program from our youth mentoring, priced because it is somebody's working day, and it is held to its own safety standard.

Not Live Yet

This program is not built or bookable yet.

This page describes the plan and the screening standard FFN has committed to before a single guide is listed or a single dollar changes hands. Same honesty standard as the rest of this site: nothing here is dressed up as further along than it is.

Two Separate Programs

Adult Guiding Is Not Youth Mentoring

FFN runs two different programs for two different ages, with two different screening standards, and they are never mixed.

Youth Mentoring

An adult matched one-on-one with a child. This requires Pennsylvania's full youth clearance stack, no exceptions, every time.

Adult Guiding

Paid, species-specific instruction on the water for grown anglers, from working pros and semi-pros. Pennsylvania's child clearances do not legally apply here, because no child is involved — but a lighter, real standard still does, below.

The adult guide list and the youth mentor list are shown separately on this site and are never blended. Nobody should have to guess which people were cleared to be near kids.

Why Screen at All

A Kid Is Going to Show Up on an Adult Trip

In an organization built on families fishing together, that is not a risk, it is a matter of time. A father books a stream class and brings his teenager, because that is what fathers do here. So the guide program is screened, and the line between "adult-only" and "a minor is on the water" is enforced in writing, not left to hope.

The Screening Standard

What Every Guide Goes Through

One basic criminal record check per guide, one time

  • A Pennsylvania State Police criminal record check, not the full youth clearance stack.
  • Runs once, before a guide is listed, funded out of the guide program rather than passed on to the guide as a hidden cost.

What disqualifies a guide

  • Sexual offenses of any kind
  • Violent felonies against a person
  • Anything carrying sex-offender registration

That list is short on purpose, and it is the whole list.

What does not disqualify anybody

  • Drug convictions, including felonies
  • DUI
  • Theft, fraud, or property offenses
  • Anything old enough that the person is plainly not that person anymore

These get disclosed on the guide's profile if he chooses, in his own words, and the angler decides who to book. Same model FFN already uses with parents and volunteers: put what is known in front of the person deciding, and let them decide.

The hard line between adult guiding and youth mentoring

  • Adult trips are adults only, stated plainly at booking with an age minimum and a real acknowledgment.
  • Any trip with a minor on it moves to the youth track: full Pennsylvania clearances and a parent present, no exceptions.
  • No guide crosses from the adult list to the youth mentor list without clearing the full youth clearance stack.
  • The two lists stay visibly separate on the site and are never blended.

Second Chances, Done Honestly

A Record Is not Automatically the End of the Story

FFN believes in second chances for people turning their lives around, kept well away from the kids' side. A guide whose record falls in the "disclose, do not disqualify" list can choose to tell his own story on his own profile, in his own words: what happened, what it cost him, what he does now, and how well he knows the water. Anglers who want to back that person can choose him on purpose. The screening is what makes that story credible instead of a rumor.
